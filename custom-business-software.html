The problem

The problem is usually a process, not a program

It starts as a spreadsheet someone clever built. Then a second one. Then a shared folder of them, a few macros nobody dares touch, and a person who is the only one who knows how it all fits together. Orders get retyped from one system into another. Someone checks specifications against a catalogue line by line. Reports take a day to assemble because the numbers live in three places.

Off-the-shelf software rarely fixes this, because the process it is meant to support is specific to your company. What fixes it is a small, purpose-built tool that does exactly that job, on the hardware you already have, and is simple enough to hand to a new hire.

Project example / Building materials

Checking specifications without the line-by-line.

A building materials supplier was checking product specifications by hand: someone would open the job requirements, the product data, and the order, and compare them line by line. It was slow, it depended on a few experienced people, and a miss was expensive.

We built a spec-check tool around the way that work was already done. It takes the requirements and the products in question, flags what does not match, and produces a record the team can send on. The people who did the checking by hand were in the room for every review, which is why it fit on the first day.

We keep client work private, so this is described in general terms. We are happy to walk through it in a conversation.

Questions

Things people ask about business software.

Desktop program or browser tool: which do we need?

If the work happens at a few fixed desks with local files, a Windows or Mac program is often simpler and faster. If several people in different places need it, or it has to run on phones, we build it for the browser. We will recommend one after seeing the process.

Will it work with the software we already use?

Usually, yes. Most modern systems, from accounting to CRM to supplier portals, have a way to read and write data. Connecting them is a large part of what we do.

What happens when we need a change later?

The code is yours and documented. We can stay on for updates, or hand it to your own people. Either way, a small tool built for one process is much easier to change than a big platform.

Do you work outside construction and building materials?

Yes. That is where we have the most depth, but the same approach applies to any company with a process that has outgrown its spreadsheet.
